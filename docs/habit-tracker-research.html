01 Why habits inside NeverExpire?

NeverExpire's core promise is "we remember so you don't have to." Today that applies to documents. Habits are the same promise applied to daily life.

NeverExpire already owns the hardest parts of a habit product:

  • A trusted reminder engine. Users already rely on the app to nag them about things that matter (passport renewals, visa expiries). Extending that trust from "yearly critical reminders" to "daily supportive reminders" is a natural brand move.
  • Daily-open motivation. A document tracker is opened a few times a month at best. A habit tracker is opened every day. This feature could transform NeverExpire from a utility into a daily companion — dramatically improving retention and making the document features more visible.
  • Existing infrastructure. JWT auth, user accounts, a REST API, push-capable mobile app, an AI extraction pipeline already wired to the Anthropic API, and a glassmorphic design system. The marginal cost of this feature is far lower than building a standalone habit app.

The user problem

People know what they should do — drink water, move, read, sleep on time, take medication. They fail at when and consistently. Existing habit apps mostly fail in one of three ways:

  1. Too much setup friction — the user must design their own system before getting any value.
  2. Dumb reminders — fixed-time pings that ignore context, so users mute them within a week.
  3. Guilt mechanics — broken streaks punish users into abandoning the app entirely.

The opportunity: use AI to remove the setup friction (the questionnaire + AI planner designs the system for the user), and design streaks that forgive rather than punish.

💡 Positioning idea
"NeverExpire keeps your documents alive. Now it keeps your good habits alive too." — habits, like documents, expire when neglected. The metaphor is unusually coherent for a feature bolt-on.

02 Guiding principles

Decisions already made for this exploration, plus non-negotiables.

✅ Decided: personal scope only
Habits belong to the account owner, not family members. This deliberately diverges from the document model (per-Person). Rationale: habits are intimate and self-directed; tracking someone else's habits raises consent and nagging-by-proxy problems. Family/shared habits could be revisited later as "accountability partners" (see Open Questions).
✅ Decided: mobile-first
The feature ships on the Expo app first. Push notifications, voice input, and health data are all native-mobile capabilities. Web gets a read-mostly companion view (dashboard + manual check-in) in a later phase, if at all.
⛔ Non-negotiable: no medical advice, ever
The app may remind ("time for your 8pm tablet") but must never advise (dosage, drug names suggestions, interactions, symptoms, diagnoses). This applies to every AI-generated string in the feature. Dietary/exercise suggestions are framed as general wellness ideas with a visible disclaimer, never as prescriptions. See §12–13 for the enforcement design.

Other principles

  • Forgiving by design. A missed day bends the streak, it doesn't break it (streak freezes / "repair" mechanics). Guilt is the #1 churn driver in this category.
  • Two-tap check-in. Marking a habit done must be possible from the notification itself, without opening the app.
  • Privacy-first health data. Health data is read on-device, aggregated on-device where possible, and only summary metrics (not raw samples) ever reach the backend. Every permission is asked in context, with a plain-language explanation, and every integration is optional.
  • AI proposes, user disposes. The AI planner drafts habit plans and adjustments; nothing activates without explicit user confirmation.
  • Offline-tolerant. Check-ins queue locally and sync later — a habit tracker that fails without connectivity fails at its one job.

04 Habit classification

A fixed, curated taxonomy — not free-form tags. Classification powers the dashboard breakdowns, AI suggestions, and quote selection.

CategoryExamplesNotes
🏃 Physical HealthDrink water, move/walk, stretch, sleep by 11pm, no sugar after 8pmThe classic set; where health-data auto-tracking applies most (Phase 3).
🧠 Mental & PersonalityRead 20 min, journal, gratitude note, meditate, learn a language"Personality development" bucket from the original brief.
⏱️ Productivity & TimePlan tomorrow tonight, deep-work block, inbox zero, no phone first hourTime-management habits; pairs well with a "focus window" reminder style.
💊 Medication & CareMorning tablet, evening insulin check-in, vitamin DSpecial category: stricter reminder guarantees, zero AI-generated content, no advice. See §13.
🕌 Personal & SpiritualPrayer times, family call, charity, digital sabbathImportant for the UAE-first market; prayer-time-relative scheduling is a differentiator (see §5).
🧹 Home & Life Admin10-min tidy, water plants, meal prepLow-stakes starter habits; good for building the check-in reflex.

Each category carries metadata used across the feature: an icon/emoji (consistent with the app's new emoji icon system), a color token from theme.js, a default reminder tone (gentle vs. firm), and an AI-suggestion eligibility flag (off for Medication).

Struggling vs. thriving

Classification is what makes the "where am I struggling / where am I doing well" view possible. Per category and per habit, compute a rolling 14-day completion rate:

  • Thriving ≥ 80% — celebrate it, consider suggesting a level-up (e.g. 10 min reading → 20 min).
  • Wobbling 50–79% — the interesting zone; AI coach targets its suggestions here first.
  • Struggling < 50% — the app proposes making the habit easier (smaller scope, better time slot) rather than pushing harder. Shrinking a habit is presented as smart, not as failure.

05 Habit creation & reminders

The MVP core. If reminders aren't excellent, nothing else in this document matters.

Creating a habit

Three entry paths, in order of shipping:

  1. Template gallery (Phase 1). ~40 curated habits ("Drink water", "Read", "Stretch", "Walk 10 min"…) with sensible pre-filled schedules. One tap to add, then tweak. This kills setup friction for 80% of users.
  2. Custom habit (Phase 1). Name, category, schedule, target (boolean "did it" vs. quantity "8 glasses"), reminder times.
  3. AI / voice creation (Phases 2–3). Describe a goal, get a drafted habit plan; or say it out loud. See §9 and §10.

Schedule types

TypeExampleStreak semantics
DailyDrink water every dayClassic daily streak
Specific weekdaysGym Mon/Wed/FriStreak counts only scheduled days; Tuesday can't break it
N times per weekRead 4×/week, any daysWeekly quota; flexible and forgiving
IntervalStretch every 2 hours, 9am–6pmCounts "windows hit today"; ideal for water/movement
Times per dayMedication 3×/day at fixed timesEach slot tracked separately; used by the medication category

Reminder behavior

  • Actionable notifications. Every reminder carries Done ✓ and Snooze 30m actions (notification category actions in expo-notifications). Two-tap check-in without opening the app.
  • Quiet hours. Global do-not-disturb window (default 10pm–7am) that all non-medication reminders respect.
  • Anchored scheduling. Beyond clock times, allow anchors: "after Fajr", "30 min after my usual wake time", "at lunch". Prayer-time anchoring (via a local calculation library — no network needed) is a genuine differentiator for the UAE/Gulf market and costs little.
  • Escalation only where it belongs. Habit reminders fire once (plus optional snooze). Medication reminders may re-fire after 15 min if unacknowledged (see §13). Nothing else nags twice — nagging kills notification permission.
  • Smart batching (later). If three habits land at 9:00pm, send one combined "Evening check-in — 3 habits" notification instead of three pings.
💡 Design detail worth stealing
The best habit apps make the check-in itself pleasurable — a satisfying animation, a haptic tick, the streak counter visibly incrementing. Budget real design time for the "done" moment; it's the emotional core of the loop and pairs perfectly with the new glossy 3D icon-puck style already in the mobile app.

06 Dashboard, statistics & streaks

The daily home for the feature. Answers three questions in one glance: What's left today? Am I consistent? Where do I need help?

Layout concept (mobile)

  1. Today strip — horizontally scrollable cards for today's habits, sorted by next-due time. Tap to check in. Completed ones sink to the end with a ✓.
  2. Streak hero — the single most motivating number (current best active streak) with a flame/plant visual that grows with the streak.
  3. Consistency heatmap — GitHub-style month grid of overall completion. Universally understood, dense, and glanceable.
  4. Category breakdown — per-category completion rings (daily/weekly/monthly toggle), feeding the thriving/wobbling/struggling classification from §4.
  5. Insight card — one rotating card: "You complete 'Read' 3× more often when reminded at 9pm vs 7pm" (rule-based at first, AI-written in Phase 2).

Statistics catalogue

Completion rateDay / week / monthPer habit, per category, overall

Best streakAll-timePreserved forever; "personal record" framing

Forgiveness mechanics (important)

  • Streak freeze: 2 auto-applied freezes per month — a missed day consumes a freeze instead of resetting the streak. Duolingo proved this mechanic dramatically reduces churn after a miss.
  • Repair window: yesterday's habit can be checked in until noon today, marked as "late" (counts for streak, flagged in stats for honesty).
  • Vacation mode: pause all habits for N days without penalty. Travel is a top habit-abandonment trigger, and this user base literally tracks passports.

07 Motivational quotes

Small feature, cheap to build, high perceived warmth — if it stays contextual and never spammy.

  • Where quotes appear: (a) inside reminder notifications as the subtitle line, (b) on the dashboard as a daily quote card, (c) on streak milestones (7/30/100 days) as part of the celebration screen.
  • Contextual matching: quotes are tagged by category and moment — a persistence quote after a broken streak, a health quote on a water reminder, a focus quote on a deep-work habit. A generic random quote feels like a fortune cookie; a matched one feels like coaching.
  • Source strategy: Phase 1 ships a curated local bundle (~200 quotes, tagged, reviewed once by a human — public-domain and properly attributed). Phase 2 can add AI-generated personalized encouragement lines ("Day 12 of reading — you've read more this month than all of last month"), which are actually stats-driven observations rather than quotes, and land much harder.
  • User control: a single toggle to turn quotes off entirely. Some users love them; others find them saccharine. Respect both.
  • Localization note: for the UAE market, include wisdom-tradition and Arabic-proverb options in the curated set; tone matters culturally.

08 Onboarding questionnaire

An interactive, conversational intake that turns "I want to be healthier" into a concrete, personalized starter plan. This is the front door to the AI planner (§9).

Design shape

Not a form — a chat-like, one-question-per-screen flow with tappable chips, ~3 minutes total, skippable at any point ("Just let me add habits manually"). Progressively disclosed: early answers prune later questions.

Question domains

💡 Output contract
The questionnaire's output is a structured user_profile JSON (goals ranked, constraints, chronotype, anchors, tone preference). This exact object is the input to every AI-planner call — which means the questionnaire can be improved, re-taken, or partially edited without touching the AI layer. A "retake / update my profile" entry lives in settings; profiles go stale as life changes.

Re-engagement questionnaires: beyond onboarding, short 3-question check-ins ("How's the plan feeling — too easy, right, too much?") every few weeks keep the profile current and give the AI coach honest signal that completion data alone can't provide.

09 AI planning & execution

The differentiator. NeverExpire already runs an Anthropic-powered extraction pipeline; this reuses that muscle for coaching. The AI has three jobs, shipped in this order.

Job 1 — Plan generation (Phase 2)

Input: the user_profile from §8. Output: a draft plan of 3–5 starter habits — deliberately few. Each proposed habit comes with schedule, target, anchor, and a one-line "why this, why now" rationale. The user reviews the draft as editable cards: accept all, tweak any, or reject. Nothing activates without confirmation.

Job 2 — Adaptive coaching (Phase 2–3)

A periodic (weekly) analysis pass over completion data + profile, producing at most one suggestion at a time:

  • "You've hit 'Stretch' at 3pm only 20% of the time, but you're very consistent at 8am. Move it?"
  • "Reading has been at 100% for 3 weeks. Ready to go from 10 to 20 minutes?"
  • "'Gym 5×/week' is at 30%. Dropping to 3×/week would let you build a real streak first."

Suggestions arrive as dismissible cards on the dashboard — never as push notifications (unsolicited AI pings feel invasive; on-dashboard advice feels available).

Job 3 — Conversational assistant (Phase 3, with voice)

Free-text/voice: "I keep failing my evening reading, what should I try?" → grounded response using that user's actual data. This is the most expensive and most guard-rail-sensitive job; it comes last.

Architecture sketch

mobile ──POST /api/v1/habits/ai/plan──▶ Flask ──▶ Anthropic API (claude-sonnet-4-6 or newer)
                                          │   system prompt: coach persona + hard rules
                                          │   input: user_profile + habit stats (aggregates only)
                                          │   output: strict JSON (tool-use schema) → validated
                                          ▼
                              draft plan / suggestion cards (never auto-applied)
  • Server-side only. API key stays on the Flask backend, same as the extraction pipeline. Mobile never talks to Anthropic directly.
  • Structured output. Use tool-use/JSON schema so the model returns machine-checkable plans, not prose to parse. Validate every field (times sane, categories valid, count ≤ 5) before showing anything.
  • Hard guardrails in the system prompt and in code: medication category is excluded from all AI inputs and outputs at the code level — not just prompt level. Any output string mentioning dosage/drug names in suggestion contexts is rejected by a post-filter. Defense in depth, because prompts alone are not a compliance strategy.
  • Cost control: weekly batch analysis, cached plans, and a small model (Haiku-class) for quote-matching and insight phrasing; the bigger model only for plan generation and conversation. Estimated cost per active user per month is pennies, but meter it from day one.

10 Voice interface

Scoped tightly: voice is for creation and confirmation — not a general voice assistant.

Two interactions

  1. Voice creation. Mic button on the Habits screen → user says "Remind me to drink water every two hours during work" → speech-to-text → the AI planner parses it into a structured habit draft → visual confirmation card ("Drink water · every 2h · 9am–6pm · Mon–Fri — correct?") → tap or say "yes" to save. The confirmation step is mandatory; voice parsing will mishear, and a silently wrong reminder schedule destroys trust.
  2. Voice check-in. From a reminder notification or the today screen: "done", "skip today", "snooze an hour". Small vocabulary, high reliability.

Technical route

  • Speech-to-text: expo-speech-recognition (community module wrapping the native iOS/Android speech recognizers) keeps recognition on-device/OS-managed — no audio ever leaves the phone to our servers. Requires microphone + speech-recognition permissions, requested in context on first mic tap.
  • Understanding: the transcribed text goes through the same AI parse endpoint as typed input — one NLU path to maintain, voice is just another keyboard.
  • Feedback: expo-speech (TTS) can read back the confirmation for eyes-free use, off by default.
  • Arabic/English: OS recognizers handle both; the parse layer is language-agnostic since the model handles multilingual input natively. Worth testing early for the UAE market.
⚠ Expo managed-workflow check
expo-speech-recognition needs a config plugin (works with EAS builds / dev clients, not plain Expo Go). This is fine — the project already anticipates dev-client builds — but it makes voice a Phase 3 item by dependency, not just by priority.

11 Native health data

Auto-completing habits from the phone's health store turns tracking from a chore into magic: the walk habit checks itself off.

Platform reality

PlatformStoreRouteNotes
iOSHealthKit@kingstinct/react-native-healthkit (or similar) + config plugin, EAS buildPer-datatype read permissions; user can grant steps but deny sleep
AndroidHealth Connectreact-native-health-connect + config pluginHealth Connect is standard on Android 14+; ships as an app on 13-

What to read (deliberately minimal)

  • Steps / distance → auto-complete "walk / move" habits when the daily threshold is met.
  • Workouts → auto-complete exercise habits when any workout session is recorded.
  • Sleep duration → auto-complete "sleep by 11pm / 7h sleep" habits.
  • Mindfulness minutes (iOS) → auto-complete meditation habits.

Explicitly not reading: heart rate, blood glucose, medications, reproductive health, clinical records. Reading less than we could is a feature — it keeps the permission dialog short and the privacy story clean.

Permission & privacy design

  • In-context asks. The permission prompt appears only when the user links a specific habit to a data type ("Auto-track this from your steps?"), never as an onboarding wall.
  • On-device evaluation. The threshold check (steps ≥ 8000?) runs on the phone. The backend receives only the resulting check-in event — identical in shape to a manual tap. Raw health samples never leave the device. This one design choice removes most compliance surface (and is the honest thing to do anyway).
  • Graceful denial. Every auto-tracked habit falls back to manual check-in if permission is denied or revoked. No dead ends.
  • Store policy note: Apple and Google both scrutinize health-data usage. App Store review will require a privacy policy update, purpose strings, and (Google) a Health Connect declaration form. Budget review-cycle time in Phase 3.

12 Dietary & exercise suggestions

The most sensitive pillar after medication. Framed strictly as general wellness ideas, never personalized health prescriptions.

What it is

  • A library of habit-sized suggestions: "add a vegetable to lunch", "swap one soda for water", "10-minute walk after dinner", "5-minute morning stretch routine (with illustrated steps)".
  • Filtered by the questionnaire's lifestyle answers (activity level, time available) — not by medical data.
  • Delivered as browsable cards in the template gallery ("Ideas for you") and occasionally by the AI coach when the user's stated goal is fitness or energy.

What it is not

  • No calorie targets, no meal plans "for weight loss", no macros, no supplement advice, no exercise prescriptions based on health conditions, nothing that responds to medical information — because we don't collect medical information (§8 deliberately excludes conditions).
  • Every suggestion surface carries a persistent, non-dismissable footer: "General wellness ideas — not medical, dietary, or fitness advice. Consult a professional for guidance specific to you."
  • The AI coach's system prompt forbids generating novel dietary/medical content; it may only select and schedule items from the human-reviewed suggestion library. This "AI picks from a vetted menu, never cooks" pattern is the key safety design of this pillar.
💡 Regional angle
Ramadan mode: during fasting, hydration and meal-timing habits auto-shift to iftar/suhoor windows and daytime water reminders pause. Almost no competitor does this well, and for the UAE market it signals the app truly gets its users.

13 Medication reminders — no advice, by construction

Valuable and asked-for, but designed as a dumb, reliable alarm clock with a checkbox — deliberately the least intelligent part of the feature.

Scope

  • User enters: a label (free text — "morning tablet", "Vitamin D"), times per day, and times. That's all. No drug database, no autocomplete of medication names, no dosage fields beyond a free-text note. The moment the app has a structured drug model, it invites interaction-checking expectations and regulatory classification as a medical device in some jurisdictions. A label and a clock time keep it firmly a reminder app.
  • Reminders are the most reliable in the app: they ignore quiet hours by default (user-overridable), re-fire once after 15 minutes if unacknowledged, and show a missed-dose indicator on the dashboard — indicator, not advice ("Missed — see your notes", never "take it now" or "double next dose").
  • Adherence history (taken/missed log) is exportable as a simple list — useful for the user's own doctor conversations, at their initiative.

Hard rules (enforced in code, not just policy)

  • Medication habits are excluded from every AI code path: never sent as AI input, never the subject of AI suggestions, never mentioned in AI-generated insight text. Enforced by filtering at the query layer, so no prompt change can leak them.
  • No quotes, no gamification, no streak-freeze mechanics on medication items — a "streak" framing on medication trivializes it and forgiveness mechanics misrepresent adherence. Plain taken/missed record.
  • A one-time acknowledgment on first medication habit: "NeverExpire reminds you — it never gives medical advice. Always follow your doctor's and pharmacist's instructions."
  • Voice creation for medication is allowed (it's just label + times) but voice confirmation reads back the full schedule and requires visual confirm — mishearing a medication time is the one place voice error is unacceptable.

14 Phased roadmap

Each phase is independently shippable and delivers standalone user value. Sizes are rough build-effort tiers, not calendar promises.

Phase 1 · MVP  The reliable core

Goal: a habit tracker people actually use daily. Effort tier: M

  • Habit CRUD with template gallery (~40 curated habits) + custom habits
  • All five schedule types; local notifications with Done/Snooze actions; quiet hours
  • Streaks with freeze + repair mechanics; vacation mode
  • Dashboard: today strip, streak hero, heatmap, category rings (daily/weekly/monthly)
  • Habit classification (6 fixed categories); struggling/thriving computation
  • Curated motivational quotes (local bundle, contextual tags, off-switch)
  • Backend: habit + checkin models, REST endpoints, offline check-in queue

Success gate: ≥ 40% of users who create a habit are still checking in after 21 days.

Phase 2 · Intelligence  Questionnaire, AI planner, medication

Goal: the app designs the system for the user. Effort tier: M–L

  • Onboarding questionnaire → user_profile; retake/update flow; periodic 3-question check-ins
  • AI plan generation (draft → confirm) + weekly adaptive coaching cards
  • Stats-driven personalized encouragement lines (replacing generic quotes where data allows)
  • Medication reminders (isolated category, hard AI exclusion, re-fire, adherence export)
  • AI cost metering + guardrail post-filters

Success gate: users who complete the questionnaire retain measurably better than those who skip it; zero guardrail violations in logged AI output.

Phase 3 · Senses  Voice + native health data

Goal: reduce interaction cost to near zero. Effort tier: L (requires EAS dev-client builds + store review cycles)

  • Voice habit creation and voice check-in (on-device STT → shared parse endpoint → visual confirm)
  • HealthKit / Health Connect integration: steps, workouts, sleep, mindfulness → auto check-ins, evaluated on-device
  • In-context permission flows; graceful manual fallbacks; privacy policy + store declarations
  • Conversational AI assistant (text + voice), grounded in the user's own aggregates

Success gate: auto-tracked habits show higher 30-day retention than manual equivalents; voice parse accuracy ≥ 95% on the confirmation screen.

Phase 4 · Guidance  Wellness library + regional polish

Goal: from tracker to companion. Effort tier: M

  • Human-reviewed dietary & exercise suggestion library ("AI picks from a vetted menu")
  • Ramadan mode; prayer-anchored scheduling polish; Arabic content pass
  • Web companion view (dashboard + manual check-in)
  • Exploration: accountability partners / shared family challenges (revisits the personal-only decision with a consent-first design)

16 Technical considerations

Notifications (the load-bearing wall)

  • Local, not push, for reminders. Habit reminders are scheduled on-device with expo-notifications — they fire offline, cost nothing, and need no server fan-out. Push stays reserved for the document-expiry features.
  • OS limits are real: iOS caps pending local notifications at 64 per app. A user with 10 interval habits can exceed that fast. Mitigation: schedule a rolling 48-hour window and re-plan on every app open / background task — a solved problem, but it must be designed in from day one, not patched later.
  • Android 13+ needs runtime notification permission and exact-alarm permission for to-the-minute medication reminders (SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM). Ask in context; degrade to inexact alarms for ordinary habits.
  • Notification actions (Done/Snooze) work in both platforms via notification categories; the response must be handled both in foreground and via the background handler.

Architecture fit

  • Follows the existing 3-layer mobile rule: HabitsScreen → useHabits (context) → habitService → ApiService. New nav routes in routes.js; all colors/spacing from theme.js; emoji icon system already in place fits the category icons naturally.
  • Backend follows the blueprint pattern: routes/habits.py, repository functions in db/repository.py, aggregations in db/queries.py. The 90-day threshold constant discipline (backend ↔ mobile sync) has a sibling here: streak/forgiveness rules must live in one place — recommend backend-computed, mobile-displayed.
  • AI endpoints reuse the existing Anthropic client setup from the extraction pipeline (extractor.py patterns), with a separate system prompt module and post-filters.

Feature flag

Ship behind a habits_enabled flag (per-user or global config) so the PoC audience can test it without exposing every account. Cheap insurance for a feature this size.

Open questions

  • Monetization: is Habit Tracker part of the core free offering, or the anchor of a premium tier (AI coaching + health sync as paid)? Affects Phase 2 architecture (entitlement checks).
  • Accountability partners: the personal-only decision is right for v1, but "share my streak with my spouse" is a proven retention lever. Revisit in Phase 4 with consent-first design?
  • Quote licensing: curated bundle must stick to public-domain / properly-licensed content — needs one review pass before ship.
  • Prayer-time anchors: local calculation vs. user's preferred prayer app's times (they differ slightly by method) — offer calculation-method setting?
  • Web companion: worth building at all, or is this feature 100% mobile forever?
  • Wearables: Apple Watch / WearOS check-ins are the natural Phase 5 — out of scope for this doc, but the data model (check-in source field) already leaves room.
  • Analytics: which product metrics do we log (privacy-respecting) to judge the Phase 1 success gate — and where do they live?
⚠ The single biggest decision
Phase 1 is deliberately buildable with zero AI and zero native modules — pure CRUD + local notifications + good design. If Phase 1 doesn't hit its retention gate, Phases 2–4 shouldn't be built. Committing to that discipline up front is the difference between a focused bet and a two-month detour.
